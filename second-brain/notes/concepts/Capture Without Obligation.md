**Saving a source must create no obligation to process it, or the cost of saving rises until you stop saving.**

## Core idea

The fear of missing out on information is not really a fear of missing the
information. It is a fear of the *backlog* - of a queue that grows faster than
it drains and turns into a standing reproach. People stop capturing not because
capture is hard but because each capture silently adds a task [inferred].

The fix is to sever the link. A captured source is a finished act. It is
searchable the moment it lands, which is most of its value; distillation into
notes is a separate, optional, later decision. This is why the systems that make
capture cheap also have to make processing explicitly opt-in - if the agent
processes everything automatically, the pile is gone but so is the judgment
about what deserved a note [inferred].

The rigorous end of the design space arrives at the same conclusion from the
opposite direction, through a *compilation-value gate*: create or expand a page
only when the source adds durable synthesis, navigation, a decision, or a
reusable connection beyond the captured source; a concise, searchable source may
need only its ledger record or a no-op, and paraphrasing merely to create pages
is explicitly prohibited
[S-20260803-claude-obsidian-transactional-knowledge-system]. Most captures
should end at capture. That is what makes the backlog harmless rather than
shameful.

## Key points

- Capture is complete on its own; the pile is insurance against forgetting, and
  its value is that it is searchable, not that it is processed [inferred].
- Auto-processing everything converts a harmless pile into a vault full of
  material nobody chose to keep, which is the slop failure
  [S-20260803-critiques-ai-maintained-knowledge-vaults].
- The compilation gate is the check on the other side: not every source earns a
  note, and declining to write one is a correct outcome
  [S-20260803-claude-obsidian-transactional-knowledge-system].
- Consequence for tooling: a health report may count the backlog, but must never
  present it as a task list [inferred].
